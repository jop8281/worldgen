# Spec calls: world-helpdesk (YOS-21 workflow, YOS-27 seed and tests, YOS-39 tasks)

Precedence as in world-helpdesk-model.md: a work order's Acceptance beats research/helpdesk-expected-behaviour.md, and the table fills every gap the Acceptance leaves.

- Call: escalate_ticket on an escalated ticket returns 409 already_escalated, so level 2 is reached only by the escalation_timeout job (urgent tickets after 60 minutes at level 1). The table's manual L1 -> L2 escalation (T6) and its 409 max_escalation are dropped. Why: YOS-21 Acceptance 1 requires 409 already_escalated "when already escalated". Reversible: yes, escalate_ticket can promote L1 to L2 and keep already_escalated for level 2.
- Call: action names are assign_ticket, escalate_ticket, resolve_ticket and reopen_ticket, with paths /tickets/{id}/assign, /escalate, /resolve, /reopen. assign_ticket is added beyond the Acceptance. Why: the Acceptance names escalate, resolve and reopen; the table's assign (T2, T3) is the only way assignee_id (readonly) gets set outside escalation, and the easy task needs it. Reversible: yes.
- Call: status, priority and POST /tickets stay as the model unit left them (status and priority writable, plain create route), and no create_ticket or change_priority action is added. Why: the unit keeps the existing entities and routes; YOS-39 Acceptance 4 names "PATCHes status to escalated" as a decoy, which needs a writable status. Graders never accept a status PATCH, because they check the on-call assignee and the escalation level. Reversible: yes, a later unit can make status readonly and replace the routes with actions.
- Call: the sla_breach job gives a ticket created through the plain POST /tickets its due time (sla_started_at plus the tier policy) at the next firing, before it flags breaches. Why: a plain create cannot compute the policy target, and without a due time such a ticket could never breach. Reversible: yes, drop the pass once ticket creation is an action.
- Call: a PATCH of priority does not recompute sla_due_at. Why: no change_priority action (see above); the job cannot tell that a priority changed. Reversible: yes, with a change_priority action.
- Call: escalate_ticket and reopen_ticket refuse a blank reason with 400 input.invalid, the same status and code the engine uses for input validation. Action refusals use not_found, invalid_state and specific codes (already_escalated, no_oncall, agent_inactive, no_change, unknown_agent 422). Why: the table's input-validation codes predate the engine's 400 input.invalid. Reversible: yes.
- Call: the medium and hard tasks target high-priority tickets, not urgent ones as the YOS-39 Acceptance examples say. Why: sla_breach auto-escalates breached urgent tickets, so a seed consistent with that job has no open breached urgent ticket (table G5), and an urgent target would let the job do the task once 15 minutes of engine time pass. High-priority breaches are only flagged, so a human decides, and no task depends on per-call ticks. Reversible: yes.
- Call: the hard task's list (open, high, breached) has 35 rows over two pages, with 7 enterprise targets of which 3 are on page 2; the grader scores the fraction of targets escalated to level 1 and assigned to the on-call agent, behind a collateral gate. Why: YOS-39 Acceptance 1 asks for two pages; a fractional score makes every strict prefix of the solution score k/7 < 1 and the first-page-only decoy 4/7. Reversible: yes.
- Call: every grader gates on ctx.changes(): any change other than to the target tickets, or a created ticket_event of a target, scores 0. Why: YOS-39 Acceptance 3. Events are allowed because the actions write them. Reversible: yes.
- Call: the medium grader gives 0.7 for the right escalation and 1 only when a new escalated event's note matches /\bsla\b/i. Why: follows the table's task 2; the word boundary stops words like "translation" from counting. Reversible: yes.
- Call: the seed has 320 tickets (new 30, open 90, pending 30, escalated 40, resolved 85, closed 45) and 60 customers, not the table's 240 tickets. Why: YOS-27 Acceptance 1 asks for at least 300 tickets; pending is in the model. Reversible: yes.
- Call: seed rows carry their own created_at and updated_at (privileged writes allow it), and every seed time is a whole minute built with ctx.time.plus from a fixed base. Why: durations cannot be negative, so "S minus 5 hours" needs a base before S; whole minutes keep the job firings (quarter hours) and the seed timeline in step. Reversible: yes.
- Call: tkt_0005 (Northwind Health, urgent, due S+10m) is an extra anchor. Why: it is the deterministic witness for sla_breach and escalation_timeout in code/test/helpdesk.test.ts. Reversible: yes.
- Call: jobs and the no_oncall guard are tested in code/test/helpdesk.test.ts with Runtime.advance, not in world tests. Why: ClientCtx cannot move the clock (table section 10, open point 2). Reversible: yes, if ClientCtx gains a test-only advance.
- Call: NOTES.md may run to 70 lines (worlds.test.ts R7), up from 40. Why: YOS-21 and YOS-39 each add a section. Reversible: yes.
